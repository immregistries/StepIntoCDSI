This page collects every place where, while building and testing a reference engine against the CDC/CDSi Logic Specification, we found a real disagreement between what the specification text (or the bundled Supporting Data) says to do and what a historical reference test case expects the answer to be - or a place where the specification simply doesn't say what to do and we had to make a judgment call. These are not software bugs in the usual sense: the code faithfully implements one defensible reading in each case, but we don't have the clinical/ACIP authority to say which reading is the intended one.

Each item below gives the patient scenario, the candidate answers with what supports each one, and the specific question we'd like an ACIP/CDSi domain expert to weigh in on. Full technical detail (code, exact Supporting Data XML, verification numbers) is linked from each item for anyone who wants it, but shouldn't be necessary to answer the question itself.

1. HepA: rounding a non-existent computed date blocking

Reference: SPEC-4.6-0026 · Business rule CALCDT-5 (Table 3-6) · FITS cases ...-HepA-AART-EXTRA-3/5/7

Patient scenario

A dose is administered on 08/29/2026. HepA's minimum interval to the next dose is "6 months." Adding 6 months to 08/29/2026 lands on 02/29/2027 - which doesn't exist, because 2027 is not a leap year.

What the specification says

Business rule CALCDT-5: "a computed date which is not a real date must be moved forward to first day of the next month." The specification gives its own two worked examples directly in the text: "03/31/2000 + 6 months = 10/01/2000 (September 31 does not exist)" and "08/31/2000 + 6 months = 03/01/2001 (February 31 does not exist)." Applying that same rule to this case gives 03/01/2027.

The disagreement

Specification's literal text and worked examples
03/01/2027 - move forward to the first day of the next month, exactly as CALCDT-5 says and as both of its own worked examples do.
Historical reference test cases (CDC's own test plan, "Extra Test Cases" EXTRA-3, 5, 7)
02/28/2027 - the last real day of the same target month, not the first day of the next one.

What we're asking: Is 02/28/2027 (the fixture's answer) actually what CALCDT-5 is supposed to produce - i.e., should the rule really round down to the last real day of the target month rather than forward to the next month's first day, and the specification's own worked examples and prose are the part that needs correcting? Or are these three historical test cases simply outdated relative to the current specification text, and 03/01/2027 (what the specification's own words say) is correct?

2. DTaP: which dose's interval rule applies at the childhood/adolescent boundary blocking

Reference: SPEC-4.6-0019 (part A) · Table 6-9 · FITS case 5eeca0522cc4517a96b0f417-DTAP-2013-0007 (and 24 similar)

Patient scenario

Born 09/01/2019. One DTaP dose given 11/01/2019, at about 2 months old. No further doses until a Tdap dose given exactly on their 7th birthday, 09/01/2026. Assessed the same day, 09/01/2026. The historical fixture's own title describes the intended scenario: "# 2 (Tdap) to child age 7. DTaP # 1 given before age 12 months. Forecast # 3 (Td) in 4 weeks."

What's actually in the Supporting Data

The DTaP/Td/Tdap schedule defines 11 numbered target doses. Dose 3's own interval rule is "4 weeks from the previous dose." Dose 9's own interval rule is "6 months from the previous dose." Separately (and not in question here), doses 2 through 7 each carry a rule saying they're not required once the patient is 7 years or older, and dose 7 separately carries a rule saying it's not required once the patient already has 2 valid doses.

The disagreement

Once every one of those skip rules is read and applied exactly as the Supporting Data spells it (a currently-dead code path is what would make dose 7's "already has 2 doses" rule start working; the age-based skip for doses 2-6 already works today), the walk through the schedule advances past doses 2-8 and lands on dose 9 as the next one to forecast - giving a 6-month interval from the Tdap dose, i.e. 03/01/2027. The historical fixture instead expects the forecast to land on dose 3, giving a 4-week interval, i.e. 09/29/2026.

Following every skip rule as written once they all work correctly
Dose 9, 6-month interval → 03/01/2027. Supported by: the age-7+ skip rule (already active today, not part of this question) plus dose 7's own "already has 2 doses" rule (currently dead code, would become active if this specific gap is fixed).
Historical reference test case
Dose 3, 4-week interval → 09/29/2026, exactly 4 weeks after the Tdap dose - matching the fixture's own title and expected date precisely.

What we're asking: For a patient who ages into the 7+ track with only one infant DTaP dose on record, is the correct next recommended dose the one 4 weeks after their most recent dose (treating this as still an early catch-up interval), or 6 months after (treating the patient as having effectively completed their available catch-up doses via the age-7+ and dose-count skip rules, landing on the longer-interval adolescent booster slot)?

Put another way: once dose 7's "already has 2 valid doses" exemption is made to work as literally written in the Supporting Data, is that the clinically correct outcome for this patient, or does that exemption need an additional qualifier (e.g., only applies if those 2 doses were given at age-appropriate times) that the current Supporting Data doesn't state?

3. Pneumococcal: minimum age with no defined grace period shipped - please confirm

Reference: SPEC-4.6-0010 · Tables 6-14, 6-15 · 5 series doses in the bundled Pneumococcal Supporting Data

Patient scenario

A patient under 19 receives a Pneumococcal dose against a target dose meant for "risk patients 19 years and older." Five such target doses exist in the bundled Supporting Data (Pneumococcal risk series for CSF leaks/cochlear implants and for immunocompromised patients).

The gap

These five target doses define an "Absolute Minimum Age" (19 years) but no separate "Minimum Age" at all. The specification's Table 6-14 gives both attributes the same independent default (01/01/1900) when left undefined, with no stated relationship between them - so "Minimum Age" defaults to 1900, a date earlier than the real 19-year Absolute Minimum Age. Table 6-15 then can't decide which of its four rules applies, because two of them both say "yes" at once. As written, this combination has no valid answer and the reference engine crashed on it.

The resolution we shipped

When Minimum Age isn't defined but Absolute Minimum Age is, we now make Minimum Age fall back to the Absolute Minimum Age itself, rather than the independent 1900 default. Practical effect: a dose given against one of these five target doses becomes fully valid the instant the patient turns 19 - no grace period, no distinction between "too young" and "not yet ideal timing."

What we're asking: Is "fully valid at exactly 19, no grace period" the clinically intended behavior for these five target doses? Or was some other resolution intended - for example, should Table 6-14 itself say Minimum Age's default is "whichever is later, 01/01/1900 or the Absolute Minimum Age" (same practical effect here, but stated as an explicit specification rule rather than an implementation choice), or should there be a genuine grace period between the absolute minimum and some other assumed minimum?

4. Any antigen with multiple eligible pathways: which status wins? shipped - please confirm

Reference: SPEC-4.6-0022 · Table 9-3 · affects RSV, and structurally also Pneumococcal, Meningococcal B, Zoster, COVID-19, HepA, HPV, Hib

Patient scenario

Some antigens (RSV is the concrete example found) can produce more than one candidate "best patient series" for the same patient at once - for instance, one series covering the general recommendation and a separate, age-gated series (e.g. "75 years and older") covering the same antigen through a different pathway. If the patient has completed one pathway but the other pathway's own series reports them as aged out or not recommended, the two disagree about the patient's overall status for that antigen.

What the specification says

Table 9-3 describes how to build a single-antigen vaccine group's forecast from its contained patient series forecast, but its own text never anticipated more than one contained forecast existing at the same time - so it gives no rule for resolving a disagreement between them.

The resolution we shipped

When more than one contained forecast exists, prefer Complete or Immune over Not Complete, and prefer Not Complete over Contraindicated/Aged Out/Not Recommended. In plain terms: if any one of the patient's eligible pathways for this antigen says they're done, report them as done, even if a different pathway (one they didn't need) says otherwise.

What we're asking: Is "the most favorable pathway wins" the intended precedence when a patient has more than one eligible pathway for the same antigen? Or should the system instead prefer whichever series the patient actually followed historically, treat the disagreement as something to flag for a clinician rather than silently resolve, or apply some other rule entirely?

5. HPV: default series vs. age-appropriate alternate for a zero-dose adult shipped - please confirm

Reference: SPEC-4.6-0021 (part a) · FITS case 675c41f8e4b089d2bdc0c483-HPV-2013-0480

Patient scenario

A 27-year-old with zero HPV doses on record is assessed. HPV's Supporting Data defines a "2-dose" series as the default pathway (intended for patients who start before age 15) and a separate "3-dose" series as an alternate, non-default pathway (intended for patients who start at 15 or older) - both within the same series group.

What was tried

  • Excluding an age-inappropriate default series entirely - this correctly handled other antigens with a similar shape (Pneumococcal, RSV, Meningococcal's age-gated groups) but left this HPV patient with no scorable series at all, since HPV's age split happens inside one series group rather than between two separate groups.
  • Actively switching to the age-appropriate "3-dose" alternate within the group - this fixed the other antigens' shape but produced the wrong recommended dates specifically for HPV.

What the reference engine actually does today - and what the historical FITS fixture itself expects - is to keep reporting the literal "2-dose" default series and its own historical dates, even for this 27-year-old with zero doses. Nothing in the specification's text describes this as intentional; it was reverse-engineered to match the fixture's own answer rather than derived from spec prose.

What we're asking: For an adult with zero HPV doses, should the system report the "2-dose" default series (as currently implemented, matching the historical fixture), or switch to the age-appropriate "3-dose" alternate? If the default-series answer is correct, is there a general rule we should write down and apply consistently - e.g., "never switch away from a series' own default status based on the patient's age alone" - so future series groups with this same within-group age-split shape behave the same way without needing a fixture to reverse-engineer the answer from?

6. RSV: is the 75+ series group equivalent to the main series group? flagged - not yet investigated

Reference: noted in SPEC-4.6-0021's "remaining gap" section · not yet its own finding

Patient scenario

A patient aged 75 or older has already completed RSV's main series (no age restriction). RSV's Supporting Data also defines a separate "75 years and older" series, in its own series group, which this patient hasn't done.

The gap

Seven other antigens in the bundled Supporting Data (HepA, HPV, Hib, Pneumococcal, Meningococcal B, Zoster, COVID-19) explicitly declare their comparable age-gated series groups as "equivalent" to each other, so completing either one satisfies both. RSV's two series groups are not declared equivalent to each other. As a result, this patient - who has genuinely completed their RSV vaccination via the main series - also gets a second, competing forecast from the 75+ group reporting them as "aged out" instead of simply irrelevant, because the system has no way to know the two groups mean the same thing clinically.

What we're asking: Should RSV's Supporting Data declare its "75 years and older" group as equivalent to its main group, matching the pattern already used by the seven other antigens listed above? Or is the lack of an equivalence declaration for RSV specifically intentional - i.e., are these genuinely meant to be two distinct series a patient might need both of, rather than two paths to the same outcome?
